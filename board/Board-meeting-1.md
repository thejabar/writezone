Agenda

To define the long-term identity, philosophy, and engineering culture of WriteZone before Version 1.0.

⸻

Background

As development progressed, it became evident that WriteZone was evolving beyond a traditional software project.

The platform required a permanent framework that would preserve its vision, engineering principles, and product philosophy as it continued to grow.

Rather than documenting decisions after implementation, the Board agreed that major systems should be designed before they are built.

This philosophy became the foundation for all future development.

⸻

Decisions

Decision 001

Blueprint Before Code

Approved unanimously.

Every major engine must have an approved blueprint before implementation begins.

This applies to every future subsystem without exception.

⸻

Decision 002

Adoption of The WriteZone Test

Approved unanimously.

Every feature must answer one constitutional question:

Will this make WriteZone a place where people spend more time, or a place where people spend their time better?

Features that increase attention without increasing value must be reconsidered.

⸻

Decision 003

Platform Philosophy

Approved unanimously.

WriteZone exists to respect the user’s time.

The platform shall prioritise:

• Knowledge

• Discovery

• Thoughtful writing

• Meaningful conversations

• Long-term trust

rather than short-term engagement metrics.

⸻

Decision 004

Repository Constitution

Approved unanimously.

The repository shall contain two equal components.

The Platform.

The Library.

The Platform contains implementation.

The Library preserves intention.

Neither is considered complete without the other.

⸻

Decision 005

Vision-Driven Engineering

Approved unanimously.

All future development follows this sequence:

Vision

↓

Principles

↓

Blueprint

↓

Architecture

↓

Implementation

↓

Testing

↓

Reflection

Implementation is no longer the beginning of development.

It is the consequence of clear thinking.

⸻

Resolution

The Board formally establishes the WriteZone Library as a first-class component of the repository.

Every future engine must include:

• A Blueprint

• Architectural reasoning

• Product reasoning

• Future evolution

• Reference to The WriteZone Test

⸻

Closing Statement

The Board recognises that software alone cannot preserve a platform’s identity.

Only principles can.

For that reason, WriteZone shall be developed with equal commitment to engineering excellence and philosophical clarity.

This meeting marks the official beginning of Vision-Driven Engineering within the WriteZone project.
